Paint one complete level of a children's adventure game, a wide picture, in exactly the same style, light and stone as the reference picture: the same castle at night, seen from above in a three-quarter top-down view, the floor seen from above and every upright thing showing its top and its front face. Take the style, the blue night light, the moonlit windows, the torches and the stone from the reference, but not its layout.

THE GRID. This is the most important rule: the whole picture is a board of exactly 20 square cells across and 12 cells down, and the grid MUST be visible, as in the reference: a thin, faint, light grid line over the whole picture between the cells, so that every cell can be counted; no numbers, letters or labels anywhere. Everything sits inside whole cells. The painting fills the whole picture to its four edges; every cell that is not floor is solid dark stone wall with its stone top, never blank.

THE VIEW. NO perspective: every edge is horizontal or vertical, nothing slants, nothing is isometric. A wall running across shows its lit top and its front face. Every door is set in the face of a wall running across and faces the viewer head-on.

THE PLACE: the night passages, higher up in the castle. The floors are corridors one or two cells wide, all joined.
1. Coming in on the left: a long corridor running across the middle left, where a stern teacher walks his round.
2. Top left: a corridor running across with a gap in its floor, one cell wide, a dark drop, and floating in the gap a single square block of pale stone glowing faintly blue, which a pupil could step onto.
3. Top middle: a small square room that is completely dark, black shadows over its floor, with a brass key on its floor and a locked wooden door with a keyhole in the face of its bottom wall.
4. Right half: the night walk, a corridor running across with a brass key lying on its floor, a locked wooden door with a keyhole set in a wall across it, a second gap with its floating glowing block, and below it a lower corridor where the teacher walks a second round.
5. Bottom right, at the very end: the way down to the dormitory, a small wooden door with a little round window, set in the face of the bottom wall.

No people, no animals and no text anywhere. Wide landscape picture.
