It is a small part of a finished painting seen from above at an angle: a corner of a castle hall's floor of big warm flagstones in the light of a late afternoon, with the lit top of a stone wall across the bottom and a wooden bookcase at the right edge. A flat colour was laid over it as an instruction: a flat golden disc on a flat patch of pale floor colour, just above the wall's top. No flat colour may remain in the finished picture.

The flat golden disc becomes one round brass plate set flat into the floor, level with it, with a simple five-pointed star engraved on it, a plain solid star like a child draws, and a dark outline round the plate: EXACTLY where the disc is and EXACTLY as big as the disc, not one bit bigger, its edge where the disc's edge is, whole, its lower edge just above the wall's top. It is a small plate, much smaller than a flagstone. The flat pale patch round it becomes plain flagstones, the same as the floor round them, their joints going on as they do outside the patch, with nothing else on them.

Everything else stays exactly as it is, pixel for pixel: the wall's top, the bookcase, every flagstone outside the patch, the style, the outlines, the colours and the light. Same painted cartoon style, bold dark outlines, soft painted shading. No grid, no text, no people, no animals.
