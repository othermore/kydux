Write the game's opening: the first thing a child sees when they open Kydux
for the first time, before they make their wizard and before any code.

It is a story page: Markdown, three or four short pages separated by a
line with only `---`, the first page opening with a `#` title. No code at
all, no `python try` block, no mention of programming, of Python, of
instructions or of the game's buttons: the mechanics come later, taught by
Nuri at the gate. This is story only.

What it tells, following the story bible's opening:

1. A letter arrives for the child: an invitation to Arvelia, a school of
   magic in an old castle on a hill above a lake, for their first year. Show
   a few lines of the letter itself, signed by Orla, the head of the school,
   warm and a little mysterious. The child is addressed as "you".
2. The journey: the road, the village at the foot of the hill, the castle's
   towers at dusk.
3. The arrival at the great gate, alone, at dusk. It is shut. There is no
   handle, no bell, and the child has no wand and knows no magic. Something
   about the gate suggests it listens. End on this moment of curiosity, not
   of fear: the reader should want to know how the gate opens.
4. The last page invites the child to tell the reader who they are, which
   leads to the screen where they make their wizard: one or two sentences
   such as "But first: who are you?". Do not describe the screen.

The child is not yet named and has not yet chosen to be a girl or a boy: do
not use `{wizard}`; in Spanish, avoid every word that depends on the gender
(write "tú", "te", "tu primer año", never "el nuevo alumno" or "bienvenido");
do not use `{...|...}` choices at all in this text. Nuri does not appear
yet. Keep it short: each page a few sentences, for a child of eight, warm
and a little magical, never scary.
