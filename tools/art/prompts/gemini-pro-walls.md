Pixel art for a children's 2D game in a top-down oblique (three-quarter) view, like classic handheld monster-catching RPGs: the floor seen from above, walls showing their top and their front face. Not isometric. A single sheet on a flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background, a strict 4 by 4 grid of square tiles, every tile exactly the same size, separated by thin magenta gaps. Each tile is grass with a thin grey stone wall on it; the wall's top is a light grey strip seen from above and, where the wall does not continue south, its darker front face of stone bricks shows just below the top. The wall pieces must join seamlessly when tiles are placed side by side: every wall arm reaches the exact middle of the tile's edge, with the same width in every tile. Row 1 (left to right): an isolated pillar with no neighbours; a wall end open to the north (wall continues up only); a wall end open to the east; a corner joining north and east. Row 2: a wall end open to the south; a straight vertical wall (north and south); a corner joining east and south; a T joining north, east and south. Row 3: a wall end open to the west; a corner joining north and west; a straight horizontal wall (east and west); a T joining north, east and west. Row 4: a corner joining south and west; a T joining north, south and west; a T joining east, south and west; a cross joining all four sides. Crisp pixels, no anti-aliasing, a limited palette, no text.
