Write the three story pages of chapter 3, *The passages*. Chapter 2
ended at the wand bank, with a school wand borrowed and night falling
(its closing page is given). Chapter 3 happens at night in the castle's
passages, in two places: the passages, from a watchtower's gallery down
through two-door rooms to the foot of the long stairs; and, at the top
of the long stairs, the night passages, with a moving stair, a dark room
and the way down to the dormitory. Dask, a strict teacher, patrols them:
gruff and brief, never cruel, fair, and in the end impressed. Nuri goes
with the wizard and teaches the chapter's lessons. A story page is pages
of Markdown separated by `---`, the first opening with a `#` title, with
no code and no Try it; three or four short pages each, the same pages in
both languages. Never scary: the night is an adventure.

1. `content/stories/after-dark/<lang>.md`, the opening. Night; the
   wizard cannot sleep. Nuri knocks: the older pupils say the passages
   change at night, and Dask patrols them, and does not want pupils out
   of bed. A dare, and a method: look before you step. Nuri knows the
   way to the gallery, and on the last page turns to teaching: to get
   past Dask, they must learn to ask questions that have an answer, true
   or false; what follows are Nuri's lessons.
2. `content/stories/the-top-of-the-stairs/<lang>.md`, a page in the
   middle of the chapter, two or three pages. At the top of the long
   stairs, the night passages begin: darker, older, with a stair that
   comes and goes. Dask's footsteps somewhere below. Nuri whispers that
   here they need to repeat while something stays true.
3. `content/stories/caught/<lang>.md`, the closing. Just short of the
   dormitory, Dask steps out of the dark: caught. He is stern, then
   impressed: he watched them use their heads, and lets them go, this
   once. The last page sums up what the chapter taught, each idea with
   the child's word and the programmer's word together: a question with
   a true or false answer, an expression; choosing with `if` and `else`,
   a condition; repeating with `for`, a loop; repeating while something
   is true, `while`; and two spells, `kirra` for light and `osto` to
   hold a moving stair. It ends with the wizard falling asleep, at home
   in the castle at last.
