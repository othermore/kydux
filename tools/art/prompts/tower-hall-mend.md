It is a part of a finished painting seen from above at an angle: the upper half of the great hall at the top of a castle's tower, in the warm light of a late afternoon, with tall windows and banners on its back wall, a floor of warm flagstones, bookcases along its left wall and a round circle of dark stone with golden runes in its floor. Two flat colours were laid over it as instructions, in two places, and no flat colour may remain in the finished picture. Everything else stays exactly as it is, pixel for pixel: the walls, the windows, the banners, the bookcases, the circle, every flagstone outside those two places, the style, the outlines, the colours and the light.

AT THE LEFT, a flat golden disc on a flat patch of pale floor colour. The disc becomes one round brass plate set flat into the floor, level with it, exactly where the disc is and exactly as big, with a crescent moon engraved on it and a dark outline round it, like the other brass plates of this hall. The pale patch round it becomes plain flagstones, the same as the floor round them, with nothing on them.

IN THE MIDDLE, at the foot of the back wall, a flat brown shape on a flat patch of pale floor colour. The brown shape becomes a wooden reading lectern standing on the floor: a wide flat wooden top, held by one wooden post on a wooden foot, exactly where the brown shape is and no bigger. On the right part of the lectern's top, where the flat white square is, lies a very big open book, its pages blank. On the left part of the lectern's top, where the flat grey circle is, a grey cat lies curled up asleep, its eyes shut, its tail round it, on the lectern beside the book. The pale patch round the lectern becomes plain flagstones with nothing on them: no cat and nothing else on the floor.

Same painted cartoon style, bold dark outlines, soft painted shading, warm golden light. No grid, no text, no people; the sleeping cat on the lectern is the only animal.
