Take only the style from the reference, not its layout, its daylight or its outdoor scenery.

THE GRID. This is the most important rule: the whole picture is a board of exactly 20 square cells across and 12 cells down, like a board game seen from above, and the grid MUST be visible. A thin, faint, LIGHT grid line (pale cream, half transparent) is painted over the whole picture between the cells, over the floors, the walls and everything else, so that every cell can be counted; the lines are light, never dark, and there are no numbers, letters or labels anywhere. Everything sits inside whole cells: a wall is a row or a column of whole cells, a door fills one cell, a step of a stair fills one cell. The painting fills the whole picture right to its four edges: no paper, no blank or empty area, no margin, no frame. Every cell that is not floor is a solid block of dark grey stone wall, painted with its stone top.

THE VIEW. A straight top-down three-quarter view with NO perspective at all: every east-west edge is perfectly horizontal and every north-south edge perfectly vertical; nothing is drawn on a diagonal, nothing slants, nothing is isometric. A wall running across shows its lit top and, below it, its front face. A wall running up and down is seen only from above, as a straight band of stone one cell wide with a lit top. Every door is set in the face of a wall running across and faces the viewer head-on.
