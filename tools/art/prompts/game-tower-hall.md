Paint one scene of a children's adventure game. The second picture is a first painting of this very place, and the new picture is painted in exactly its style, its light and its colours, with the same things in it: seen from above in a three-quarter top-down view, as in a classic 2D role-playing game, the floor seen from above and every upright thing showing its top and its front face, which faces the viewer; painted cartoon 2D game art with hand-drawn bold dark outlines, warm saturated colours, soft painted shading, lit tops and shaded faces, chunky readable shapes, loving detail. The first picture is the plan of the place on its grid, and the new picture follows the plan: where the second picture and the plan disagree, the plan is right.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOWS. The great hall at the top of the school's tower, in the warm golden light of a late afternoon, as if the roof had been lifted off it: one square room in the middle of the picture, and round it the rest of the castle's inside seen from above, the thick stone walls and beyond them the dim neighbouring rooms with their own floors and furniture (a store of scrolls with shelves, a small study with a desk, a room with perches and baskets), closed by their walls, with no door and no opening into the hall. Nowhere is there an empty, flat or black area: every part of the picture is floor, wall or furniture, painted.

THE VIEW. The whole picture is seen straight from above at one and the same angle, like a board game on a table: no vanishing point, no converging lines, nothing isometric, no diagonal walls. Every east-west edge is horizontal and every north-south edge vertical. A wall running across shows its lit stone top as a band and, below it, its front face of fitted stone blocks; a wall running up and down is a straight band of stone seen from above, with no side face. Every raised thing has a dark outline all round it. Shadows fall on the floor as the floor's own colour darkened.

THE PLAN, the first picture, to be followed cell by cell, as if its grid lay on the floor of the new picture: the pale cells are the hall's floor of warm flagstones; the grey bands are the walls, their lit tops paler; the dark cells are the neighbouring rooms. Along the top, the back wall's front face with three tall arched windows full of golden afternoon sky and a coloured banner between each two, as in the second picture. In the middle of the back wall's foot stands the wooden lectern, the brown box of the plan, with a very big open book on it, its pages blank, and on the lectern beside the book a grey cat lies curled up asleep, its eyes shut. At the foot of the back wall, near its right end, where the plan has a small black mark, a very small arched opening built in stone, like a neat mouse hole with its own little arch, dark inside: a little door for small animals. In the middle of the floor, the big round circle of the plan, flat, level with the floor: a ring of dark polished stone with small brass runes set along it, pale stone inside, nothing standing on it. The three golden discs of the plan are three round brass plates set flat into the floor, level with it, each inside its one cell and no bigger: a crescent moon engraved on the upper left one, a sun on the upper right one and a star on the lower right one. The brown cells along the left and the right walls are low bookcases, wooden perches for birds and round baskets with cushions, standing against the side walls and only on those cells, so that every pale cell of the floor is clear. In the bottom wall, in the middle, an open stone archway two cells wide with no door leaf, the way in: the hall's floor runs through it to the landing of the winding stair below, which goes down out of sight; the bottom wall's lit top runs straight across the picture just below the last pale row, with nothing of the hall's floor below it but the archway.

THE GRID. A thin, faint, LIGHT (pale cream, half transparent) grid of exactly 20 square cells across and 15 cells down is painted over the whole finished picture, so that every cell can be counted, lying where the plan's grid lies; the lines are light, never dark; no numbers, letters or labels anywhere.

The hall has no other door or opening. No people and no text anywhere; the sleeping grey cat on the lectern is the only animal. Wide landscape picture.
